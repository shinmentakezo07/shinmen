## Correctness as a feature

Accounting has a truth condition: debits must equal credits, forever. Most
systems enforce that in application code, where a bug — a retried request, a
half-applied batch, a developer who assumed the sign — can write a corrupt
ledger and nobody notices until a close.

I modelled the invariant in the database itself, so no application bug, no
matter how creative, can write a corrupt ledger.

```sql
ALTER TABLE postings
  ADD CONSTRAINT balanced
  CHECK (amount <> 0) DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED;
-- plus a per-transaction sum check enforced in the same deferred window
```

Deferred, not immediate, because a transaction is written in several statements:
the debit, the credit, and the fee rows land one at a time. The constraint fires
at commit, when the whole set exists. That single word `DEFERRABLE` is the
difference between an invariant and a roadblock.

### The half that SQL cannot check

A deferred constraint proves each transaction balances. It cannot prove that
postings land in the right accounts — that is a semantic question. So the ledger
layer is a small, boring function that takes a business event and returns the
postings it implies, and every write goes through it. There is no code path that
inserts a posting directly.

<Aside label="Trade-off">
Holding the lock until commit means a long batch serialises against every other
write. For three users that is a feature, not a cost: reconciliation can never
race a payment.
</Aside>

## Shape of the thing

- **Immutable postings.** Nothing is updated or deleted; a correction is a new
  transaction that reverses the old one. The account balance is a `SUM`, not a
  stored column, so there is no denormalised number to drift.
- **Idempotent payment intake.** Stripe webhooks retry. Every event carries its
  provider id, and a unique constraint on `(provider, provider_event_id)` makes
  a duplicate delivery a no-op instead of a double charge.
- **Two-stage close.** The period closes at month end, but accounts stay open
  for adjustments for a further fortnight behind an explicit reversal, so the
  numbers people reported do not silently change.

## What was hard

**The invariant that looked obvious and wasn't.** Currencies. A cross-currency
Journal entry does not balance in every denomination — it balances in the base
currency at a specific rate. The first design checked the sum of raw amounts,
which passed every test in a single-currency fixture and failed the first time
the customer invoiced a client in USD. The constraint now sums the base-currency
amount, and the rate used is stored per posting rather than looked up at read
time, because a historical report has to use the rate from that day.

**Reconciliation against the bank.** The bank's export and the ledger disagree
constantly, for legitimate reasons: pending payments, fees applied late, a
customer paying two invoices with one transfer. I built a matching screen that
proposes candidates and requires a human to confirm. The version I wanted to
build — automatic matching with a confidence score — was wrong here: the
reconciliation was the control, and automating it removed the one place a human
looked at every movement.

**Time.** Postings are dated, not timestamped, and the date is the business day
in the account's timezone. Two days of debugging went into an invoice dated
23:58 UTC that belonged to the previous day locally and broke the close.

## Results

$2.1M processed in year one with zero unbalanced entries — a constraint check
that never fired because the design made the bad write unrepresentable rather
than merely unlikely. Six weeks from concept to first customer, invoicing on day
one, because the bookkeeping model was the whole product.

## What I would change

I over-built the reporting layer for a company with three users. The chart-of-
accounts UI took six weeks and the growth was slower than the button. Next time:
ship the ledger and one honest report, and let the customer ask for the rest.

I would also have written the reversal path in week one. It is the operation that
makes the immutable model humane, and retrofitting it meant auditing every place
I had reached for an update.
